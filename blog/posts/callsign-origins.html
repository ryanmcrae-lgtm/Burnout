LORE ARCHIVE:
The Origin of Call Signs on Korob

[ ARCHIVE RETRIEVAL // KOROB HISTORICAL RECORDS // ACCESS: GENERAL ]

Before the colonisation effort collapsed, command issued designations — serial numbers. Cold, efficient, disposable. When the supply lines went dark and central authority fractured, the survivors gave each other names instead.

Names earned from deeds.

That tradition never died.

// ---- //

THE DESIGNATION PERIOD

In the early years of the Korob Colony, personnel were identified by their colonial assignment codes. A seven-digit alphanumeric string, issued at processing, stencilled onto their kit. It told you their sector assignment, their function tier, and their arrival batch. Nothing else.

The colonists found this adequate in the way that grey rations are adequate. It sustained function. It did not sustain anything more than that.

[ ARCHIVE EXCERPT — COLONIAL LOG, YEAR 3 ]

"We stopped using the numbers around the fourth month after the orbital relay went down. It wasn't a decision. Someone just called Okonkwo-7742 'Ember' because she walked into a burning supply cache to pull out two injured engineers. The name stuck. Others followed. Command tried to reinstate designation protocols. No one complied."

THE LOGIC OF EARNED NAMES

A designation describes what you were assigned. A call sign describes what you did.

This matters on Korob for the same reason it matters anywhere that the stakes are real: when you are in a dust storm and you need to trust the person next to you, you do not need to know their function tier. You need to know who they are — what they've demonstrated, what they've survived, what they've done under pressure.

A call sign is a compressed record. Every operator who hears it knows something about the person who carries it, even if they've never met.

HOW CALL SIGNS ARE ASSIGNED

There is no formal ceremony. There is no committee. Call signs emerge the way all meaningful names emerge — from the convergence of character and circumstance.

Common sources:

  • A defining action in the field, especially one that was unexpected
  • A phrase the operator is known to say under stress
  • A characteristic that becomes more pronounced under pressure
  • Something the operator survived that they shouldn't have
  • A refusal — something they would not do, even when ordered

The call sign generator you'll find on this site approaches the tradition differently — it listens to something you did, and finds the name in it. Not the name you'd choose for yourself. The name that fits the story. These two things are rarely the same, and that gap is usually where the truth lives.

CARRYING THE NAME

An operator who's been given a call sign doesn't always like it at first. That's considered a good sign. The names that fit best are often the ones that identify something the person hadn't quite named in themselves — an edge, a tendency, a quality they'd been carrying without acknowledgement.

On Korob, your call sign is not what you aspire to be. It's what the planet already sees in you.
