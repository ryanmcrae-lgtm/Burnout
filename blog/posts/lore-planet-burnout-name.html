LORE ARCHIVE:
Why They Call It Planet Burnout

[ ARCHIVE RETRIEVAL // KOROB HISTORICAL RECORDS // ACCESS: GENERAL ]

The official designation is Korob-IV. The colonisation report called it "a promising mid-atmosphere terrestrial candidate." The people who tried to live there called it something else.

The name Planet Burnout didn't come from the heat, or the radiation, or the endless grinding attrition of survival. It came from what it does to the ones who stay.

// ---- //

THE FIRST GENERATION

The colonists who arrived on Korob-IV in the first wave were selected for resilience. High-performers. People who'd demonstrated the capacity to work under sustained pressure, to adapt, to push through. The colonial authority chose them deliberately — they needed people who could build something from nothing, under conditions that would break ordinary citizens.

They were right that those people could work under pressure. They were wrong about what happened next.

The first generation built. They built infrastructure, protocols, supply chains, social systems. They worked at a rate that would have been impossible on Earth and was only possible here because of what they were — and because of what the planet demanded. Korob-IV has a way of making everything urgent. The atmosphere, the resources, the instability. Nothing on the surface is ever fully stable. There is always something else to address.

[ ARCHIVE EXCERPT — YEAR 7 COLONIAL ASSESSMENT ]

"High-performer selection has produced the expected output gains. We note, however, an unexpected rate of what we are terming 'cognitive and motivational collapse' among the cohort. Operators who performed at extraordinary levels for the first 18 to 36 months are arriving at a threshold where output drops to near zero. They are not injured. They are not ill. They appear to have simply... stopped. We are investigating."

THE INVESTIGATION

What the investigation found was not what command expected.

The operators hadn't collapsed. They had exhausted the resource that the selection process had never thought to measure — not skill, not intelligence, not resilience. They had exhausted the part of themselves that had a reason to keep going. The internal engine that makes effort feel connected to purpose. Once it burned out, the capacity to perform remained. The will to use it did not.

One colonist's account from Year 9 describes it this way:

"I could still do the work. I could do it faster than almost anyone here. I just couldn't locate any reason to. Not fatigue — I wasn't tired. Something had gone dark. Like the light that makes work feel like it's going somewhere. I'd been running on it for so long I hadn't noticed it was finite. And then it was gone."

HOW THE NAME STUCK

Early colonists began calling it "burning out" — a term borrowed from electrical engineering, describing a component that has been run past its rated capacity until the conductor itself fails. Not broken. Not damaged. Burned through.

The planet got the name because it kept doing this to people. Not to the weak ones — to the strong ones. The ones who could push through everything until there was nothing left to push through. Korob-IV did not break its colonists. It burned them out. It consumed the fuel they ran on, until they were empty.

The survivors — the ones who found something on the other side of empty — are the reason the outpost still exists. They discovered something the colonial reports hadn't anticipated: that the burned-out state, once reached, was not the end of the story. It was a different kind of beginning.

WHAT THE PLANET KNOWS

Experienced Korob operators will tell you the planet has a character. They say it burns you out because it needs to — because the self you bring with you from the old world, the high-performing, pressure-adapted, optimised version of you, cannot actually survive here long-term. The conditions are too real for the performance. The planet strips the performance and leaves what's underneath.

Whether what's underneath is enough is what the planet is actually testing. Not your capacity. Your core.

// ---- //

Korob-IV is still its official designation. But no one calls it that. Everyone who's been here calls it what it is.

Welcome to Planet Burnout. The name is a warning and an invitation. Most places only manage one of those things.
