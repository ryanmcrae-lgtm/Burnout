SURVIVAL LOG 001:
Sector 7 Dust Protocols

[ INCOMING TRANSMISSION // KOROB OUTPOST // PRIORITY: STANDARD ]

The grey dust of Sector 7 is not just atmospheric particulate. It gets into everything — your lungs, your gear, your mind. Operators who've worked the sector for more than 30 cycles report auditory anomalies. A low hum. Sometimes voices. We are not classifying this as unusual. We are classifying it as expected.

This guide covers dust filtration, comm hardening, and the three rules that keep you breathing.

// ---- //

WHAT IS THE DUST

Sector 7 sits in the basin of the old refinery collapse — a six-kilometre crater of pulverised infrastructure, oxidised mineral slurry, and several decades of atmospheric settling. The dust is not toxic in the conventional sense. It does not cause acute poisoning. It causes something slower and harder to name.

On Earth, they called it chronic stress exposure. Here, we call it Sector Drift. The symptoms are well-documented:

  • Difficulty locating the present moment
  • Decisions that feel made by someone else
  • Loss of the ability to distinguish urgency from noise
  • A creeping sense that none of it matters — and then, eventually, that too much of it does

You are not imagining it. And you are not alone in it.

[ FIELD NOTE ]

The dust doesn't kill you. It just makes you forget why you wanted to survive in the first place. That's the more dangerous condition.

FILTRATION PROTOCOLS

Standard-issue filters are rated for 12-hour exposure in Sector 7. Experienced operators replace theirs at the 8-hour mark. The remaining 4 hours is where the drift sets in — slowly enough that you don't notice until you're lost.

Filter equivalents in non-Korob terms:

  • Physical filter: Sleep. Non-negotiable. Deprioritised sleep is a filter you've removed from your system.
  • Cognitive filter: Time that is not optimised for output. Idle time. Walks. Staring at walls.
  • Social filter: Contact with people who do not need anything from you and are not measuring your performance.

You cannot run in Sector 7 without filters. You can try. People try every cycle. They always need extraction eventually.

COMM HARDENING

High-dust environments degrade signal integrity. What arrives at your receiver is not always what was transmitted. You start responding to distorted signals as if they were accurate. You start treating non-threats as critical alerts. The threat assessment system breaks down first.

Hardening your comms means auditing what you're receiving:

  • Is this an actual emergency or ambient noise that pattern-matches to an emergency?
  • Who is transmitting this to me, and what is their interest in my response?
  • What would I do with this information if I were not in a dust environment right now?

THE THREE RULES

These come from operators who've spent extended time in Sector 7 and made it back with their cognition intact. They're not optimisation hacks. They're survival minimums.

  • Rule 1: Know your exit coordinates before you enter. If you don't know what done looks like, you'll work until you collapse.
  • Rule 2: One thing that is not the mission. Every cycle. Something you do that the dust cannot touch — because it has no use-value, no output, no metric.
  • Rule 3: Say what sector you're in. Out loud, to someone. The drift convinces you no one needs to know. That's how it wins.
// ---- //

Sector 7 is not the most dangerous place on Korob. It is the most ordinary place on Korob. That's what makes it lethal. You are already in it. The question is whether you've got your filters on.
